How it works

Ordinary days cost neurodivergent people more than they cost other people, and the cost is easy to miss until it has built up. This record makes it visible.

The scale

Each day runs from 0 to 100, where 100 means completely drained. You start each day at the level you woke up with. Each thing that costs you adds points by its size: small 5, medium 15, big 30 and huge 60. Each thing that restores you takes points off the same way. Sizes are yours: change them in your catalogue at any time, or mark something as hitting softer or harder today.

The rule

Once a day, under a minute, and skipping a day costs nothing. A tracker that asks too much becomes another drain.

Your privacy and your data

You don't need an account. There's no sign-up, no name and no email. Open the link and start using it.

Your entries are saved on your device, in your browser. They are never sent anywhere. Nobody else can see them, including the person who made the app. The app has no tracking, no advertising and no outside connections.

Each device and browser keeps its own record. If you use it on your phone and your laptop, those are two separate records. In a private or incognito window, everything is deleted when you close the window.

Clearing your browser's data deletes your record. On iPhone and iPad, Safari can also delete it if you don't open the page for a week or more. Adding the app to your home screen stops that happening.

Back up regularly. On the Record page, tap "Back up" to download a backup file ending in .json. It holds everything: your catalogue, your ratings and every day you have recorded. It is not meant to be opened, only used to restore the app. Keep it somewhere safe, such as your email or cloud storage. To move to a new phone or recover your record, open the app there and tap "Restore a backup."

You choose who sees it. "Download CSV" makes a table of your days that opens in Excel, Numbers or Google Sheets, to keep or give to a psychologist or doctor. A CSV cannot be used to restore the app. Nothing is shared unless you do it. Anyone who uses the same device and browser can open your record, so keep that in mind on a shared device.

To add it to your home screen: on iPhone or iPad, open the link in Safari, tap the Share button, then "Add to Home Screen." On Android, open the link in Chrome, tap the menu, then "Add to Home screen."

What it is: a way to see what ordinary days cost you and what gives some back. The starting ratings are estimates. Change them to match your own experience. It isn't a medical or diagnostic tool.

My plan for two heavy days in a row

Write this on a calm day. If you record 100 or more two days running, the Today page shows it to you. For example: cancel what can be cancelled, tell one person, stay home, and do only things from my "gives back" list.

What it draws on

Daily hassles research (Kanner and colleagues, 1981; Almeida and colleagues, 2002), autistic burnout research (Raymaker and colleagues, 2020), energy accounting (Toudal and Attwood), spoon theory (Miserandino) and battery theory (Becker), and lived experience. Starting ratings are estimates to adjust, not measurements. All lists are written in our own words.
